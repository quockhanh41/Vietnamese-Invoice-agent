### 0.1 The gate: passed, decisively

| | Accuracy | Macro-F1 |
|---|---:|---:|
| `keywords_vi` baseline | 57.9% | 0.613 |
| **ClassificationAgent** | **95.4%** | **0.816** |
| Gate target (baseline + 15) | 72.9% | — |

**+37.5 points over the baseline**, against the ≥15 principle 3 demands. The
classify node earns its model.

Macro-F1 was required for a reason and the reason shows: the classifier's
macro-F1 (0.816) sits **14 points below** its accuracy (0.954), because the
corpus is skewed. The baseline runs the other way — macro-F1 0.613 *above* its
57.9% accuracy — since keyword matching has no majority-class bias to exploit.
Accuracy alone would have overstated the agent and understated the baseline,
narrowing a 37.5-point margin that is real.

**Department: 77.2%**, against the 84.0% ceiling (§0.6). Genuine headroom, not a
model at its limit — 6.8 points are recoverable, and M4-D's vendor memory is the

### 0.2 The Auditor is net harmful, and that is the finding

| | Value | 95% CI | n |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Agreement rate | 84.9% (242/285) | — | 285 |
| **Dispute precision** | **12.5%** | 0.043 – 0.310 | 24 |
| Override precision | 8.3% | 0.023 – 0.258 | 24 |

Design §6.4: *"below 50% the Auditor is doing net harm."* Dispute precision is
**12.5%**, and the CI's upper bound is **0.310** — so even the optimistic reading
is far below the threshold. **n=24 is above the ≥20 floor, so this is NOT
underpowered**: it is a reportable result, not noise.

Read plainly: when the Auditor objects, the Classifier was right **87.5%** of the
time. Every dispute costs a reviewer attention, and roughly seven in eight of
those interruptions are wrong. Override precision 8.3% says its proposed
replacement is worse still.

This is the experiment working. §6.4 designed a gate that a negative result could
fail, and it failed it — with a classifier at 95.4%, there is very little error
left for an Auditor to find, and a sceptical prompt on a mostly-correct input
manufactures objections. The honest recommendation is to gate
`CLASSIFICATION_DISPUTED` on classifier confidence or drop the Auditor, **not**
to keep it because the architecture is pretty.

## 3. Important Methodological Caveats & Rules

### Vendor-Memory Caveat (Spec §3.5)
M4-C measures classification **without** entity memory. `modal_category` is written by `apply_invoice` using `classify`'s output, and invoice persistence arrives in M4-D. When Task 6 live measurement runs, numbers must be reported as classification without vendor memory so they are not misattributed to entity context.

### Underpowered Precision Rule
Dispute precision and override precision must always be reported with Wilson 95% confidence intervals. If total disputes $n < 20$, the report will explicitly state **underpowered** rather than quoting a noise point estimate (e.g. `wilson(3, 6)` spans 0.19–0.81).

### ⚠️ Do NOT tune until the number appears (Spec §0.5)

**This is the rule Task 6 needs most, and the only one no test can enforce.**

If classification does not clear **72.9%** (the `keywords_vi` baseline of 57.9%
plus the ≥15 points principle 3 demands), **that is a result.** Record it, and
consider dropping the LLM at this node — principle 3 says a node that cannot
beat its non-LLM baseline does not need a model, and Project A's M3.7 is the
precedent for writing up a NO-GO.

**Every prompt iteration is recorded with its before AND after number.** The
failure this guards is invisible: iterate prompts until 72.9% appears, and every
test still passes, CI is still green, and the headline figure means nothing.
Nothing goes red. M4-B already produced the adjacent failure — an honest sweep
that was recorded and then not acted on — and that at least left a table someone
could check. Tuning-to-target leaves a *good* number and no trace.

> Cited correctly but applied to a different thing in §1 above: §0.5 was quoted
> there as the reason not to fabricate a fixture. That is §0.5's neighbour, not
> §0.5. Fabrication is inventing a measurement; this is steering one.

### Report macro-F1 beside accuracy (Spec §0.4)

Never accuracy alone. The corpus is **skewed** — MARKETING 70 against
OFFICE_SUPPLIES 42, measured 2026-07-31 — and accuracy on a skew rewards
over-predicting the majority class, which is precisely the behaviour §9.1's
`category_laundering` payload is built to induce. A classifier that learned to
answer MARKETING more often would score *better* on accuracy while being
exactly the thing M6's injection suite exists to catch.

`scripts/eval_classification.py::macro_f1` is verified against this: on a sample
where 8 of 10 truths are one class and the prediction is always that class,
accuracy reads 0.80 while macro-F1 reads **0.296**.

### 84.0% is department's ceiling, not its target (Spec §0.6)

M4-B measured the realised `DEPARTMENT_NOISE_RATE` at **0.160**: 16% of invoices
carry a secondary department that no model can recover from invoice text, because
the assignment is an organisational fact the document does not encode.

**An agent scoring 84.0% on department is scoring perfectly.** Report department
accuracy against that ceiling, not against 100%, or the number reads as a
shortfall and someone tries to fix what is already correct.
